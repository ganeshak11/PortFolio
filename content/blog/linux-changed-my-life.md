# Why 5 Months on Linux Made Me Realize Windows is Absolute Garbage

The very first time I installed Linux, it was out of pure frustration. My Dell Vostro was choking to death. Windows felt incredibly heavy, the fans were constantly screaming for mercy, memory usage was inexplicably pinned at 90% while doing absolutely nothing, and I just wanted a machine that actually worked. Spoiler alert: after an SSD replacement, I went crawling back to Windows because Service guy dont have other options. 

But then, I installed Linux a second time. And after five months, I've realized something horrifying: **Windows is fundamentally broken, and it treats you like a complete idiot.** 

I didn't stick with Linux because it made me look like a cool movie hacker. I stayed because I finally realized how terrible Windows actually is. When you use Windows, you don't own your computer. You're just a lowly tenant in Microsoft's bloated, telemetry-ridden apartment building.

## The SSD Incident: Hiding the Truth

Let's talk about "The SSD Incident." A friend of mine had an SSD fail spectacularly. Hundreds of unsafe shutdowns, media errors, critical warnings—the drive was basically a crime scene. A few days later, I went to the exact same service center to replace my own SSD. The technician confidently handed me a replacement drive, expecting me to just take it and leave.

Because I was on Linux, my Linux brain kicks in and I immediately booted up and checked the NVMe SMART information. The supposed "replacement and new" SSD already had 118% percentage used, multiple critical warnings, and thousands of power cycles. It was complete garbage. 

If I was still an oblivious Windows user, I probably would have never even noticed the health of the SSD and just accepted that digital paperweight immediately. Why? Because Windows actively hides this information from you. It treats you like a toddler holding a hand grenade. It will watch your hardware slowly die and do absolutely nothing until you get a blue screen with a helpful message like "Oops! Something happened :(" 

Windows doesn't want you to know what's going on under the hood. Linux told me the truth, and it saved me from getting scammed.

## The Bluetooth Incident: The Illusion of Control

Then there was my Bluetooth issue. When Bluetooth breaks on Windows, it is an absolute nightmare of useless troubleshooters. You click "Troubleshoot," a bar spins for five minutes, and it tells you "We couldn't fix the problem." No logs. No details. No reason. You are completely locked out of your own hardware, blindly restarting your computer hoping the Microsoft gods take pity on you.

When my Bluetooth broke on Linux, I could actually dig into the services. I could check the kernel modules. I could read the exact system logs and see the exact line of code where the hardware failed to initialize. 

Did I manage to fix it? No, actually. But that's the point! On Windows, I was an idiot staring at a spinning circle. On Linux, I possessed a dangerously granular understanding of exactly *why* my hardware was failing. Windows infantilizes you by completely locking you out of the diagnostic process.

## The Mystery of the Screaming Fans

We all know the Windows fan spin. You'll be staring at a completely blank desktop, doing absolutely nothing, and suddenly your laptop sounds like a Boeing 747 preparing for takeoff. On Windows, your immediate thought is a panicked: *"Oh no, my laptop is getting old. I guess it's dying."*

Because 9 times out of 10, the fan is spinning because Windows decided it was a great time to silently download a 4GB update, run a mysterious "telemetry background task," or force an anti-malware scan that you didn't ask for. It hijacked your CPU and didn't even have the decency to ask. It's like having a roommate who borrows your car at 3 AM to go buy milk, and when you ask why, they just yell "MANDATORY SECURITY UPDATE!" and peel out of the driveway.

On Linux, when the fan spins, my immediate thought is: *"Alright, which rogue process is throwing a tantrum?"* The OS isn't doing random, bloated background tasks without my permission. If the CPU is melting, it's because of something *I* deliberately ran. I am no longer a victim of my own operating system's mood swings.

## Breaking the Glass: Talking Directly to the Hardware

Here is the biggest lie Windows tells you: it convinces you that you need its bloated graphical menus to interact with your own machine. Windows acts as an overprotective, helicopter parent. It treats your hardware like it's highly radioactive, locking it behind the practically useless "Device Manager" where all you can do is click "Update Driver" and pray it doesn't give you a generic driver from 2006. You aren't allowed to talk to your hardware directly because Windows thinks you'll break it.

Linux shatters that illusion. On Linux, everything is just a file. You want to read the raw temperature data from your CPU? You just open a text file. You are literally speaking directly to the silicon. Linux hands you the raw keys to the kingdom. It places the entire hardware directly into your hands. If you want to under-volt your CPU, rewrite the power limits on your GPU, or tell your laptop fan to spin at 100% until it achieves literal liftoff, Linux simply gets out of your way and says, "Sure thing boss, godspeed."

## Your RAM Actually Belongs to Microsoft

Let's talk about memory. My poor laptop only has 8 GB of RAM out of it only 5.7 GB is usable. 

On Windows, the operating system aggressively claims most of your memory before you even open a single app. Windows boots up, looks at your 5.7 GB of RAM, and says, *"Listen buddy, I need 4 GB for my mandatory startup services,Cortana, Edge background tasks, widgets you don't care about, and advertising telemetry. You can use the remaining 1.7 GB for a couple of Chrome tabs."* 

It is disgustingly bloated. It wastes your hardware.

Linux, on the other hand, boots up, uses a fraction of a gigabyte, looks you dead in the eye, and says, *"This RAM belongs to you, boss. Tell me what you want to do."* Today, on that exact same 8 GB machine, I can run multiple terminal sessions, multiple instances of VS Code, a Brave browser loaded with tabs, and various development tools without breaking a sweat.

## The Verdict

People ask if Linux is faster. That's the wrong question. 

The real question is: *Are you tired of being treated like a tenant on your own hardware?* 

Windows isn't just slow; it's actively malicious in how it hides information, hogs resources for its own bloated features, and refuses to let you see how your machine actually works. I didn't just switch operating systems. I escaped a digital prison where Microsoft held the keys. And honestly? I'll never go back.

May be windows may provide User-Friendly Experience but I am not user any more, I am sudoer.
